# Picker

Search, list and the delivery bar ([design](../design.md#the-model)). The Inspector and Reader are in [inspector-and-reader.md](inspector-and-reader.md); copying, pasting and notices in [delivery.md](delivery.md).

## Native macOS app

Built from the system's parts ([macOS recipe](macos.md#the-native-recipe)). Like Spotlight it is one field, one list and one footer line; the maintainer chose it from A/B mock-ups on 2026-11-28.

- **Window:** 640 pt wide at 100 % text, not resizable. Its height follows the content: the field alone, then the list up to eight rows, then the footer.
- **One session, the engine's:** the engine holds the query, results, highlight, the references added with their details, the format and the preview ([0066](../decisions/0066-native-shells-use-system-parts.md) point 3). The app sends intents and draws each reply, so what the footer shows is always what ↩ delivers.
- **Field:** the bird in the lead column, then the query in 22 pt type, with no frame. Drag the field's row to move the window. The field always reads typed citations: `;` separates references, and a page, signal or note typed with the search ("see shore 417") becomes that reference's details, unless the words match a title whole. A hint line under the field says how Ibis reads it ("p. 417, “see” before").
- **List:** section headings in small secondary type. Rows have two lines: the title, then the byline that fits whole, the year and the match reason. Compact rows are 44 pt, comfortable 52 pt. The highlighted row takes the system selection colour while the keyboard acts on it. Hovering shows Add and Peek on the row. Holding ⌘ numbers the wholly visible rows and ⌘1 to ⌘9 cite one. Click selects, ⌘-click adds and double-click delivers.
- **Several references:** ⇥ (or ⇧↩) adds the highlighted result, with a light haptic, and clears the field; its row shows a check. The footer counts them: **N References** opens a menu with each reference's page, Move Earlier, Move Later and Remove, then Keep My Order and Undo. ⌫ in an empty field removes the last. ↩ with a new search delivers the added references and the highlighted result.
- **Page, prefix and suffix:** ⌘L, or a click on the citation in the footer, opens a popover with the locator menu and field, Before, After and Show as. Esc closes it.
- **Footer:** the format pop-up, the citation exactly as ↩ delivers it, the delivery (the target app's icon and name, or Copy) with ↩, and Actions ⌘K. ⌥↩ copies instead. A problem or notice takes one line above it, with at most one fix. After a delivery the menu bar bird nods (not under Reduce Motion).
- **Actions (⌘K):** a real `NSMenu` of every command that can run now, grouped, with its key equivalent and toggle state.
- **Peek (⌘Y):** its own non-activating glass panel beside the picker, as Quick Look shows a file, with the kind, title, every author, journal, year and citation key. Typing goes on while it is open.
- **Paste permission:** asked only at the first paste. A card beside System Settings names the switch, and its app icon can be dragged into the list. It closes itself once access is on; until then citations are copied for ⌘V.
- **Empty states:** `ContentUnavailableView` with one primary action.

The sections below describe the web picker and its design targets.

## Search bar

- Order: the bird in the lead column, the query on the title column, the format pop-up ending on the trailing edge. No clear button: Esc clears, as in Spotlight and Raycast, and every search field (the picker's and a view's) is the same frameless field. The header is its tallest control plus 2S, with a divider below [C2]. Its controls take the field's height, so the gap above and beside them is S and their corners nest in the window's (`--r-control`).
- `#tag`, `y:2019-2022` and `a:name` plus a space become removable chips; Backspace in an empty box removes the last.
  Remaining (F9): expose the engine tokenizer's filter identities and spans to
  the picker, then use them for chips and the shared empty-Backspace path.
- **Keep Window Open** [C6] (⇧⌘P, or in More): a choice for this session, so it has no button of its own in the header (control placement, maintainer 2026-11-10). While it is on, a pressed pin sits beside More in the bar, and a click on it turns it off. Esc never unpins.
- The format pop-up names the format alone ("APA", "Pandoc"): where the citation goes is the bar's action, and whose format it is (this app's, a project's, this paste's, your default) is the palette's heading and the pop-up's spoken status. An editor request shows its own format as plain words. One output has one name everywhere, the engine's (decision 0035). Hidden until a library is chosen. It opens the **format palette** ([decision 0042](../decisions/0042-formats-apps-and-writings.md)): the way back first when the format isn't yours, recent formats, the app's own choices, New project… (once offered: [delivery](delivery.md#copying-and-pasting)), then the catalogue (key formats, citation styles, and the ways to share references, which hold for one copy: [delivery](delivery.md#copying-and-pasting)), also found by typing; More's Change Format… is found by "share", "export", "BibTeX" and "DOI" too. Beside the list, key formats preview the chosen references, citation styles show a labelled example, and a way to share references shows exactly what it would copy of the shown citation, asked of the engine without replacing what Enter copies. Projects directly in the list and real style previews remain the decision's target.

## Results

- **List and row** [C4]. The kind glyph, title and byline share the header and bar’s columns. Compact is the default, with 32 pt rows and a fixed byline column beside the title. Comfortable uses 44 pt rows and puts the byline directly below the title. Highlights paint the same row bounds in both densities.
- **One record summary** (#1675): the row, `ibis search`, `ibis show` and agents read a record's title, authors, year and container from the engine's one summary, so a record shows the same year everywhere. The year is the date's year (also when a source writes it as text, "2016"), else the source's own date text cut to 20 characters ("circa spring 1890s"), else "No date". A year written as text is found by searching for it.
- **Kind and citation membership**. The type glyph stays in place through hover and selection. A reference in the citation has a check at the trailing edge. Add a reference with ⇧↩, ⌘-click or More.
- **Byline**. The engine sends ordered alternatives so the row can fit whole parts. It starts with one or two full names, or the first surname and “et al.” for three or more. It drops the container, tries surnames and shortens the author part at word boundaries. The year or “No date” stays whole. The drawer and Peek show the full byline, and assistive text includes every full name, kind and citation key.
- **Peek** (⌘Y, as Quick Look by keyboard; Space types a space in the search): the highlighted row's whole record in the drawer above the bar, which takes its room from the list's end, so it covers no row and the row stays in view: title, every author's full name, the full container or publisher, year, type and citation key. It earns its place beside the opened row: the row cuts its title to a line and never shows the type or key. It never takes focus, so typing carries on; ⌘Y again, Esc, or a new highlight closes it.
- **Words win** (maintainer, 2026-11-18): when the words as typed match a title, key or number in the library whole, they stay search text and nothing is read from them, so a title's number ("Room 22", "Nineteen Eighty-Four 1984") never silently becomes a page and a title opening with see, compare or contra ("See You Later") keeps it. One notice line says so ("Kept as words. For a page, add a comma: “room, 22”."). A comma asks for the page: "room, 22". The engine decides (`kept_words` in its search answer).
- **Typed with the search** (#1762): a signal before the words ("see", "cf.", "see also") and a locator after them ("vale 417", "vale ch 2", "vale pp. 417-419") become the reference's prefix and locator when it is added, shown as one token beside the query ("see · p. 417"); × on the token searches as typed. A bare page after a space needs two digits or a range and never follows a label word, so "modern art 3" and years stay words. The engine reads it (`ibis_core::shorthand`), so `ibis search --shorthand` and the MCP search tool's `shorthand` read the same. When a person reaches for a token's details by hand (focusing a token, or opening details a second time in an opening), one line in the notice slot teaches it with a word that finds the first cited reference, the person's own ("“aster 417” for a page, …"), never a sample word, at most three times on a computer. It belongs to that opening: closing the picker retires even a pending hint reply.
- **Drag the citation**: the bar's beak is the citation's proxy, as a Mac window's title icon is its document's: drag it into any app to drop exactly what Enter pastes (plain text, and rich text for a formatted style). It drags only what Enter would deliver: not while the citation is being made or is out of date, and never a refused one.
- **Hover** [C5] only tints a row under a moving pointer; a click selects, so reaching across rows for Copy never changes what is copied. Double-click delivers.
- **List scrolling**: only keyboard navigation reveals the highlight; pointer hover never scrolls. Rows sliding beneath a still pointer during scrolling do not change the highlight until the pointer moves. A clicked result stays under the pointer and only that row consumes the click, even if results re-render first. Arrow keys take back ownership.
- **Empty search** [C11]. A new library centres “Type to find a reference” and its search hint. The empty search otherwise shows up to three works in **Cited here recently**, followed by as many ordinary **Recently added** rows as fit, up to twelve. When there is space left, one quiet search hint sits in its centre.
- **Paste Reference List** (maintainer, 2026-11-16): once the app Ibis came from has citations from this run of Ibis and the format is a style, the empty search ends with one row for it, on the list's columns (`ChoiceList`'s `compact`, as Use Your Own Library), with how many references and where ("The 3 references you cited in Word since Ibis opened"); More lists it too. Choosing it puts those references in the bar's citation, each as its entry, in the order the style gives its list, (the Reference list form), and delivers them as Enter does: pasted, or copied with the reason, when the app only copies ("Copy Reference List"). The engine counts them for the app (`recents::List::cited_here`), so the row never shows for an app with none; Remove, Reset List, Undo and Forget Recent Citations apply to it as to Cited Since Opening. Key formats offer none: there the document's own bibliography is the list.
- **Typing keeps the picker still**: rows, headings and the bar stay until the next reply; nothing blanks. A click on a row from an earlier query does nothing.
- No matches: "No matching references", "Try a surname, year or fewer words.", then Clear Search, and beside it, quiet, the library itself (**Open thesis.bib ⌘O**, **Open Zotero**) to add what is missing: a miss always has a next step. When something else is the obstacle the screen names it, with its one fix: a filter (**Remove Filter** keeps the plain words) or "Not in your libraries" for a pasted citation. Left-out Zotero libraries are where else to look, not the obstacle: the title stays "No matching references", the line says some Zotero libraries are left out, and the fix is **Search All Zotero Libraries**. A source failure and an empty library keep their own screens. When nothing matches every word, closest matches show under **Closest matches** and keep a "Closest match" pill on the citation.

## Search rules

Built in `ibis-core` so every front end shares them (#1204).

- Any word order; case, accents and letter variants folded (`mueller` finds Müller); "et al." and "and" ignored; every word must match.
- Per word: whole word, then word start, then inside a word (three or more characters, or CJK), then one typo in words of five or more letters. Numbers, years, identifiers and tags are never guessed.
- Field hints: `a:` people, `t:` title, `j:` journal, `k:` key, `#` tag, `y:` year or range; quotes keep a phrase. An exact citation key always matches.
- Ranking: exact key, exact title, matches as typed, then typos, then abstracts; field weight; newest year; stable order.
- **Pasted lists** (#1326): keys in any supported citation syntax, or identifiers, in paste order under "Pasted list"; missing ones under "Not in your library: …". Up to 1,000 entries or 64 KiB; otherwise ordinary search.

## Delivery bar

- **One band** [C7] [C8]. The bar sits under a hairline on the window’s surface. The beak aligns with the kind glyphs, and the citation starts on the title column. A library button precedes the delivery action and the fixed More ⌘K column. The healthy library uses its glyph with the source in its tooltip and menu. A library needing attention uses words. The action and More use the same borderless controls and keycaps.
- **Said once**: the header's pop-up names the format; the action names where the citation goes and what Enter does, never the format and never a mode word beside it. Every outcome of Enter has its words, from one function (`features/picker/enterLabel.ts`, which a test walks for every outcome and destination): "Paste into Word ↵", "Copy ↵" ("Copy 2 Citations"; "Copy Entry" and "Paste 2 Entries into Word" for a numbered style), "Send to Editor" for an editor's request, "Open PDF, or show item in Zotero" in Find mode, "Paste Here to Try" for the first screen's practice, "Change Citation" for the last one pasted; "· your order" follows when the person's order is kept. A paste that needs the system's permission is still a paste: Enter tries it, and the copy it falls back to says what to allow.
- **Refused**: a citation Ibis must not deliver (a key that names a different work in the bibliography, decision 0028) keeps its action's words with the action off: dimmed, its ↵ cap without the accent, still focusable and spoken, described by its reason. The reason is a line of the bar above the citation, in plain words with its mark in the tile column and its fixes (Retry, Locate Bibliography…) at its end; that line takes its room from the list's end. Enter then delivers nothing, and a screen reader hears the reason again. Nothing else shows the refusal.
- **Tokens** (decision 0045): the line is exactly what Enter pastes, and each reference is a token of its own words in that output, on a soft fill, with a small quiet × that appears on hover or keyboard focus and removes it (with the named Undo). The brackets and separators the output adds stay plain, quieter text between tokens ("(" [Okafor & Lin, 2019 ×] "; " [see Vale, 2021, p. 417 ×] ")"; Pandoc's "[" [@vale2021, p. 417 ×] "]"). A token whose words do not name its reference (`1` in IEEE) adds its handle beside them, the first author and year ("1 · Aster 2026"); a token longer than a third of the line, as a note style's sentence is, ends in "…", with its words in full in its tooltip and details. Neither the × nor the fill is pasted. When the line does not fit, it scrolls sideways and keeps the focused token in view; there is no second copy of the text.
- **Keys on the line**: ⌘↓ in Search goes to the last token; ← → walk the tokens; ⇧← ⇧→ move the focused reference one place; ⌫ or ⌦ removes it, with Undo that puts it back in its place with every field; ↓, Space or a click opens its details; ↑ or Esc returns to Search. Enter pastes, wherever focus is. The citation itself is never a focus stop while it has tokens. A focused token shows the ring every button has; the token whose details are open keeps the pressed fill.
- **Order** (#1762): the line always shows what Enter pastes, in the order Enter pastes it. Moving a token turns **Keep My Order** on, so the move shows even in a style that sorts; the bar's action then says so ("Paste into Word · your order"). Keep My Order is in More, and at the foot of the details when the style sorts. Keys outputs always keep the order. `ibis cite --keep-order` and MCP `keep_order` match.
- **Motion** (shared motion tokens): a token settles in when added and slides when the order changes, the details grow from their token, and the line updates in place without a flicker. Reduce Motion cross-fades. No motion delays Enter, a paste or a key.
- On copy a closing beak mirrors the opening one and a "Copied" capsule draws its check; Reduce Motion shows the end state. Each copy is confirmed once, in one place.
- The line holds one line in its own box: an output written on several lines (BibTeX) reads as one, its words in full in each token's tooltip, and the slot clips anything taller, so the bar never grows. A token's details say only what is about that reference: the output's note and that reference's own warnings (its closest match, a field the output cannot show, a key missing from the writing's bibliography). Each warning carries the reference it is about from the engine, so none shows under another token; the citation's warning count keeps them all together and stays put while details are open.
- Pending output dims, then "Formatting…"; Enter waits. A failed format says why and offers another style; refusals stay visible until corrected. Every preview ends in a result or a stated failure: an engine stop is asked again once (after the destination read, when one is running), and a second stop says the citation could not be prepared, so Copy is never left waiting on "Formatting…".
- The bar keeps the last citation through a running search and when the next search finds nothing.
  The picker session owns that offer independently of result rows, including its
  prepared result. Enter waits for a running search; a no-match reply cancels
  that queued Enter. Fresh Enter can copy the retained offer. A failed search, missing pasted list or
  changed library snapshot cannot revive an earlier offer.

### The library in the bar

- **Library** [C8]: healthy, the library asks nothing of the person, so the bar shows its glyph alone, between the citation and the action, its name in the tooltip and the spoken status; the library menu opens from it as before (Reload, Search in, the Zotero profile, Choose Library…). When it needs the person (checking, none chosen, entries skipped, a problem) its words show there instead, with a status dot in its colour only for look and stop, and its fix beside them. With no citation it starts on the leading edge, the glyph in the tile column.
- **Going back** ([decision 0047](../decisions/0047-opening-references-placement-and-back.md)): Esc goes back from palettes and sheets, and closes the Inspector. Empty Backspace goes back only in palettes, after the key is released and pressed again; in sheets and the Inspector it only edits. ⌘[ on macOS, Alt+Left elsewhere and mouse Back return from all three, keeping sheet drafts. IME composition and pop-ups take precedence. Every return restores the parent's search, selection, scroll and focus; reopening a form restores its draft and place. Tab keeps its existing palette and sheet behaviour.
- **More** (⌘K) opens the **actions palette**, the one list of the picker's commands: every action, grouped by what it acts on (the library, this citation, Ibis), each with its key, and found by its name, a common word for it ("preferences" finds Settings) or its key's description in the keys sheet ("open settings"). It is the third door to every action (control placement). The search field has no command mode: whatever is typed there, ">" included, searches the library.
- **More never moves**: it has the last column of its own, so nothing about the action moves it: not a longer label, not Alt, not the action being absent. A click aimed at More can never land on Copy. The action sits against it and grows toward the status. The status always keeps room for its fix ("Find thesis.bib…"), so the fix never wraps onto the action, and the citation keeps `--bar-citation-min` before anything folds; a failure line under them spans the bar.
- Every hint is label first, then keys, on one baseline. When the bar does not fit, shorten the status, but never below about a word (`--status-short`, so never "Z…"); then truncate the action's label with "…", its keys and full name kept; and only by the window's width drop hint labels to keys, which happens before the status would be cut further (`canvas/geometry.ts`). The action's own width never decides that fold. With ⌥ held, its two actions cannot both truncate on one line, so their labels fold to keys when their full names do not fit; More keeps its label either way.
- **Quiet bands**: nothing in the bar is a filled button but a refusal's fixes. The main action reads as Raycast's: its words in the text colour at medium weight and the bar's one accent on its ↵ cap, so the eye rests on the results, not on the bottom edge. The library status, the source action, More, the reference count, notes and Details are quiet.
- Never show an action twice on one screen. With ⌥ held on macOS, or Shift held over the bar on Windows and Linux: "Add to citation ⇧↵", the Esc step, then More. Shift in Search keeps typing capitals without changing the bar. Source opening is ⌘O / ⌥⌘O on macOS and Ctrl+O / Ctrl+Shift+O elsewhere ([decision 0047](../decisions/0047-opening-references-placement-and-back.md)).
- **One place for each status**: library state lives only in the bar's library status; a citation refusal only in the bar's reason line; settings notices, failed actions and anything passing stand in for the list's heading line (see [Notices](delivery.md#notices)).
- **Delivery agrees**: the header's format, the bar's action and the keys sheet read one delivery answer (`citationDelivery`, `enterAction`). An editor request shows its own format as plain words, with **Send to Editor** in the bar; format and project choices, including their commands and keys, stay hidden or inactive until that request ends.
- **One reading of a library's health**: the dot, the hover text and the library menu all draw from `libraryCondition`. A problem in words is never a green dot: anything the menu reports makes the dot at least Look.

| Case | The bar's library status |
| --- | --- |
| Starting, first run | "Checking library…", "No library chosen" |
| Loaded | The library glyph; "Zotero" or "thesis.bib" in its tooltip; the count is in the library menu's heading |
| Changed while open | The glyph alone; the update time stays in the detail |
| Reloading | The glyph alone while healthy; the detail says "reloading…"; rows and the citation stay until it ends |
| Entries skipped or to check | "damaged.bib · 4 entries skipped · 1 to check", look dot; the hover text and the library menu's heading say why and where: "4 entries skipped: 2 use the key good1 (lines 3 and 9), 2 could not be read (lines 21 and 25). Check line 15." |
| Problem, old copy kept | Warning dot, never the stop red, since the old copy is still searched; "Zotero not updated · 2 h", then one fix button with its key ("Open Zotero ⌘O", "Find thesis.bib…") |

- Counts include bibliographic records only. Background work shows a small progress ring before the words.
- **Failed refresh with a last-good copy** (planned, maintainer, 2026-11-04): keep a coherent snapshot of the same established source searchable and citable. The persistent source status clearly says that refreshing failed and gives the last successful read time; it clears after a successful refresh. Enter keeps the action the bar promises, without a repeated confirmation or a forced switch from paste to copy. This does not permit a citation whose source identity is uncertain or whose selected work or key binding is known to have changed (decision 0028).
- The status opens the **library menu** above its selector (`top end`), spanning it and ending where it ends, with a clear gap so it never covers the selector: a heading with the library, its count and age, then a separator; with several Zotero profiles and Zotero not running, a **Zotero Profile** heading with one checked row each; with several Zotero libraries, a **Search in** heading, All Libraries, then one checked row per library; a library that could not load stays dimmed with "Couldn’t load"; then, after a separator, Choose Library… ⇧⌘O, Use Zotero for a file, and Reload Library.
- **Open the source** (⌘O): the words always name the target. While opening Zotero is the fix, ⌘O opens Zotero, so the fix button and More both read "Open Zotero ⌘O". **Show in Zotero** selects the reference in Zotero (starting it if closed); **Open thesis.bib** opens the file; **Open Zotero** only when Zotero is the source and not running. ⌥ turns "Open thesis.bib" into "Show in Finder". Library-level reveal exists only for a file: Zotero offers no Finder action or Option variant in the bar, and ⌥⌘O leaves the view alone. Never reveal Zotero's data folder. If the source cannot open, offer Choose Library… or Find….

## Keys

`KEY_TABLE` and `DEFAULT_PICKER_SHORTCUT` in [`apps/desktop/src/keys.ts`](../../apps/desktop/src/keys.ts) define shortcut actions, platform bindings and the default shortcut to open Ibis.

- The shortcut to open Ibis follows the active layout’s letter.
- Double-clicking a row or the bar's action pastes or copies what the citation shows; success hides an unpinned window. The tile’s + adds to the citation, in order.
- Numbered-result shortcuts act on the first nine rows wholly in view, renumbered on scroll. Holding the command key for one second writes each row’s key where its type icon was, as plain secondary text ending at the tile column: no cap and no plate, nothing moves, and the year stays. A cited row’s check gives way for the hold too (the bar shows the citation) and returns when the command key is let go. They work only while the result list is shown, not over a palette, sheet or empty state.
- Escape steps back one layer from any control: sheet, menu, inspector, library chooser, then clears search (the citation stays), then hides. A held key takes one step.
- The question-mark shortcut works only outside a field. Typing outside a field goes into search, except in the reader’s pages.
- ⌘I opens the focused citation token's details, or the last offered token's
  details from Search. The key registry supplies its binding in More and the
  shortcut catalogue.

## Window behaviour

- **Opening**: on the focused window's display (the app being written in), horizontally centred, with the **search row's centre at a quarter of the usable height**, clamped so the whole frame fits. Usable space excludes the menu bar, notch safe area, Dock and taskbar on any edge, using that display's scale. macOS uses the frontmost app's focused window; Windows uses the foreground window's monitor; X11 uses the active window where available. Unknown focus falls back to the pointer's display. On Wayland Ibis requests the same placement and accepts the compositor's choice. Opening has a short fade and focus in search. A search from under a minute ago returns selected, unless Esc or Clear cleared it: an explicit clear sticks. Text typed while opening is kept. The window is current in its first frame.
- **Where Ibis opens**: Settings › General offers Centre and Top left, Top right, Bottom left and Bottom right. Centre keeps the search anchor above; corners keep 16 logical pixels from the usable edges (less where the whole frame would not fit). A remembered deliberate move on that display wins; Reset Window Position uses the selected placement without changing size. The choice is portable, applies on the next opening, and stays unwritten at Centre.
- **Dropping a file**: one `.bib`, `.biblatex`, CSL-JSON, `.ris` or `.csv` file dropped on the picker (a `.txt` RIS export only through Choose Library) becomes the library, and the bar's library tooltip names it; its detail keeps the count. Anything else leaves the library alone and says what Ibis accepts until the next good drop or opening.
- **Moving and resizing**: drag the bird or the header's empty room (never the search field or a control) to remember a position for that display, as fractions of its usable width and height. Display identity is the macOS display UUID, Windows monitor device path or X11 output name, so rearranging screens retains their positions; a changed work area clamps the whole frame into view. Automatic relocation from an unplugged display preserves the destination's remembered spot or default. Old name-and-origin entries are ignored. **Reset Window Position** (also double-clicking the bird or the header's empty room) clears that display's remembered spot and restores the default placement, keeping the current size; Undo restores the spot. The window keeps one size while open; content does not resize it. Deliberate edge drags and window-size choices still set its size. Without stable display identity (including native Wayland), size choices last until quit and are never saved to settings.
- **Other windows**: a command that hands the screen to another window has one effect however it was reached, and Esc then belongs to that window. Settings always closes the picker, even a pinned one, which would otherwise float above it; the pin stays for the next opening. An opened source hides an unpinned picker; pinned, it stays beside. A file chooser belongs to the picker, which stays behind it.
- **Clicking outside** hides Ibis and the click reaches its target, unless pinned or a dialog, drag or file chooser is open.
- Overlays close when the picker hides and do not reopen. Tooltips appear after 600 ms and never take focus.
- **Never** take keys people type with (so not Ctrl+Space or Ctrl+Alt), open by itself, appear in the Dock, taskbar or Alt+Tab (only Settings and a pinned picker do), or animate past 280 ms. On X11 the first press over a full-screen app is ignored and a second within two seconds opens Ibis.
- **Platform limits**: macOS a non-activating floating panel, also over full-screen apps; Windows copies instead of typing into elevated apps; Wayland copies and says "Press Ctrl+V" ([compatibility](../compatibility.md#linux-shortcuts-and-tray)).
- Hidden Ibis waits for events; nothing polls while it is hidden.

## Opening observation

On summon, Rust captures the target and builds one typed `PickerSnapshot` with
library status, resolved recents, destination and format, and interface
preferences. The webview applies it synchronously and acknowledges that
presentation before the native window is revealed. A later presentation replaces
the whole observation; old rows and previews cannot flash while its query runs.
Launch follows the same path, retaining the snapshot until the listener exists.
