For the moment between an idea and its source.

Ibis is a small, free app† for finding references in Zotero or a BibTeX file. A quick route from your library to the place you write, on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Get the early preview or try it right here @how

† Still an early preview, built in public. Expect rough edges, and tell me what you need: it helps decide what comes next.

@how

From your library to your sentence

A citation key is a short label for a reference, such as okafor2023. Tools like Pandoc, LaTeX and Typst use it to find the full reference.

  1. You need a source.

    Wading birds notice changes in water quality before most instruments do

  2. Press your Ibis shortcut. Search by author, title, year or journal, or combine them, and choose with the arrow keys.

    Press Enter. Ibis copies the key.

  3. You paste it. Pandoc, LaTeX or Typst builds the reference list.

    Wading birds notice changes in water quality before most instruments do [@okafor2023].

Ibis finds and copies the key; it does not generate your document’s bibliography. It also copies formatted citations in APA, Chicago, Harvard, IEEE and MLA.

@download

Try the early preview

Free, for people happy to meet rough edges: it is not a stable release. The apps aren’t signed by Apple or Microsoft yet, so your computer warns you the first time you open Ibis. Read the installation notes for your system before you download.

Mac

  • Mac with Apple siliconDisk image
  • Mac with IntelDisk image

Which Mac? Choose Apple menu → About This Mac. “Chip: Apple M…” means Apple silicon; “Processor: Intel” means Intel.

  1. Open the disk image and drag Ibis to Applications.
  2. Open Ibis. If macOS says it can’t check the app, make sure you downloaded it here, then choose System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

Windows

  • WindowsInstaller, 64-bit
  1. Run the installer. If the WebView2 Runtime is missing, it downloads it once.
  2. If SmartScreen warns you, check you downloaded it here, then choose More info → Run anyway, if offered. Smart App Control or a work computer may block it: keep protection on and ask IT, or wait for a signed build.

Linux

  • AppImageMost distributions, 64-bit
  • Debian or Ubuntu.deb package, 64-bit
  • NixOS or NixInstall with Nix
  1. Allow the AppImage to run as a program, then open it. Some systems need FUSE support.
  2. On Wayland, open Ibis from your app launcher; its shortcut isn’t available there yet.

Files come from GitHub. Release notes · Ibis Nightly, with newer unfinished work, installs as a separate app.

@setup

Two steps to your first citation

  1. Choose your library

    For Zotero, open Settings → Advanced and turn on “Allow other applications on this computer to communicate with Zotero”. Keep Zotero open while using Ibis. Or choose a .bib file; Better BibTeX can keep one up to date.

  2. Press your shortcut

    By default it’s Control+Command+R on macOS, and Ctrl+Shift+Y on Windows and Linux X11, if it is available. Type a few words, choose with the arrow keys and press Enter, just like in @how.

Ibis reads your library without changing it. Searching and copying work offline after setup; update checks are opt-in. App privacy · Full installation guide.

@why

A reference within reach

I wanted a simple way to find references in Zotero or a BibTeX file without setting up several extra apps. I move between operating systems, so I wanted the same tool to work consistently on Windows, macOS and Linux.

My goal is to make Ibis fast, easy to set up and easy to customize for different ways of writing. It is still early. I’m building it for myself and other people who want their reference tools to make life easier, and I’d like your help shaping it.
